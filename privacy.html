Privacy Policy

version: 2 (8 October 2026, revised 14 October 2026)

This policy explains what data Routine Clash collects, what it is for, who it is shared with, and what you can do about it. It is written from what the app actually does, not from a generic template.

In case of any discrepancy, the Portuguese version of this document prevails.

Who is responsible

The controller of your data is Samuel.

For any privacy question, or to exercise the rights described below, write to suport.routineclash@gmail.com. We undertake to reply within one month.

What we collect, and why

To give you an account

  • Email and password, or your Google account if you sign in that way. They let you sign in and recover access. The password is never stored as text: it is hashed, and not even we can read it.
  • A username and a short code, so friends can find you. The username is public within the app.
  • Date of birth. It confirms the minimum age and puts your birthday on the agenda. It cannot be changed once set — that is a database rule, not an oversight, and it exists so nobody can change it to collect the birthday reward more than once a year.
  • An avatar, drawn inside the app itself.

To make the app work

  • Your tasks, goals, lists and events: what you write, when they happen, how often.
  • What you complete, with date and time, and the XP, levels and streaks that follow from it.
  • Proof photos, when you take them.
  • Your profile picture, if you set one.

For the game

  • Your game progress: your bixos, chests, items, weapons, island, coins and diamonds. It is kept on your phone, with a copy on the server so you do not lose it when you change phones.
  • What you do in the modes with other people: the damage you dealt to your group's boss and, in PvP, your trophies, the team you chose to defend with, and the result of each fight.
  • Diamonds and coins cannot be bought with money. The app has no purchases or payments of any kind.

For the social side

  • Who your friends are, the groups you belong to, who you blocked, and the colours you assigned to each person.
  • The messages you write, in private and group conversations.
  • Any reports you file, so we can moderate.

For alarms and notifications

  • A device identifier (notification token), so notifications know where to go. It is deleted as soon as you sign out.
  • Your phone's brand is read so we can show you the right permission instructions. It is never stored — it is used and forgotten.

Screen time (Android only, and only if you want it)

If you create a screen-time goal, Android asks you for a special permission so the app can see how long you use other applications.

In that case only two things go to the server: which application you chose to measure, and how many minutes you used it. Nothing else. The list of applications you have installed, and the time you spend in all the others, stays on your phone and never leaves it.

You can withdraw this permission at any time in Android's Settings.

On what basis we process this

  • To deliver what the app promises (performance of a contract): the account, tasks, XP, friends, messages. Without this data there is no app.
  • With your consent: notifications and screen time. You can withdraw it in your phone's Settings without losing anything else.
  • Legitimate interest: reports and account security measures, so the app stays a safe place.

Who else sees your data

We do not sell your data to anyone, and we do not use it for advertising. The app has no advertising and no behavioural analytics.

There are five recipients, and no others:

  • Supabase — hosts the database, the accounts and the files. Your data sits on its servers, in the Europe (Paris, eu-west-3) region — that is, inside the European Union.
  • Google (Firebase) — delivers notifications, and handles sign-in if you choose your Google account.
  • Cloudflare (Turnstile) — the "I am not a robot" check when you create an account and when you sign in. It receives your IP address and technical characteristics of the browser inside the app, only to decide whether there is a person on the other side. It does not receive your email, your password or anything you do in the app. Cloudflare may process this data outside the European Union, with the safeguards the GDPR requires for such transfers.
  • DiceBear — draws the avatar images. When the app shows an avatar, it asks this service for the image, which receives your IP address and the drawing options (colours, clothing and a "seed"). On a new account that seed is your username, which is already public in the app anyway. It receives nothing else.
  • Other people using the app, which is the part most worth understanding:

What others see

  • Your profile picture, name, code and level are public: anyone with an account can see them, friend or not.
  • The rest of your profile is friends-only, and you decide what to show in the privacy settings: your activities, your statistics.
  • A task you share is seen by the friends you choose, along with the proof photo if you took one. A task you keep to yourself is seen by nobody.
  • In a challenge or a group task, participants see that you did it, at what time, and in which place you finished.
  • In the group boss, the group's members see your name and how much damage you dealt that week.
  • In PvP, whoever gets you as an opponent sees your name, your trophies and the team you left defending (its bixos, their weapons and items).
  • The achievements you choose for your profile showcase are visible to anyone with an account, like your photo and name. You can show none.
  • You only join the weekly global ranking if you accept. If you do, anyone with an account sees your name, your photo and the XP you earned that week. You can leave whenever you want.
  • Your friends can visit your island, only to look: the island and the bixos walking on it. When you visit a friend's island, they see your name and photo among the visits of the last 24 hours.
  • Messages are read by whoever receives them. They are not end-to-end encrypted: they are stored on the server, and it would technically be possible for a database administrator to read them.

How long we keep it

  • Proof photos are deleted after 24 hours. The record that you did the task stays — it still shows that you did it and the XP you earned — but the image disappears from the server.
  • Everything else stays while you have an account.
  • When you delete your account, we delete everything: profile, tasks, completions, messages, groups and photos. Your username becomes free for whoever wants it.

Your rights

You have the right to know what data we hold about you, to correct it, to delete it, to restrict what we do with it and to object to processing. In practice:

  • Delete everything: Profile › My account › Delete account. It is immediate and cannot be undone.
  • Correct: the username can be changed in Profile, every 30 days. The date of birth cannot be changed, for the reason given above — if it is wrong, write to us.
  • Access your data or take it with you: Profile › My account › Export my data. It copies everything we hold about you to the clipboard, and you can paste it wherever you like. If you would rather we sent it another way, write to suport.routineclash@gmail.com.
  • Withdraw consent: notifications and screen time, in your phone's Settings.

If you believe we have not handled your data properly, you can complain to your country's data protection authority. In Portugal that is the CNPD (www.cnpd.pt).

Minors

Routine Clash is not for children under 13. We ask for a date of birth when the account is created and do not allow accounts below that age.

If you know of an account belonging to someone under 13, write to us and we will delete it.

Security

  • Connections to the app are encrypted.
  • Passwords are stored hashed and cannot be read by anyone.
  • Who may read each piece of data is decided by the database itself, not by the app: even a modified version of the application still could not see what is not its own.
  • Proof photos live in private storage that only you and your friends can reach, through a temporary link that expires.

No system is perfect. If you find a flaw, please write to suport.routineclash@gmail.com before making it public, and thank you.

Changes to this policy

If we change anything important, the app will tell you and ask you to accept again. The version is at the top of this page.
